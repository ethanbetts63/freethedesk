# What matters most for small business SEO in Perth?

Most small business websites in Perth don't need SEO tricks. They need four basics done properly: a clear title on every page, headings that say what the page is about, a page for each service, and a Google Business Profile set up with care. Each takes a few minutes to check. This guide explains each one, how to check yours and what good looks like, then lists what can wait.

## What is a page title, and why does it matter?

The page title is the text in a page's `<title>` tag. It is usually what Google shows as the blue link in its results. Google says the title link is [often the primary piece of information people use to decide which result to click](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).

Google doesn't always use the title you wrote. It builds title links from several sources, including the `<title>` element, the main visible title and headings such as the `<h1>`. A 2022 Zyppy study of 80,959 titles on 2,370 sites found Google [rewrote 61.6% of them](https://zyppy.com/seo/google-title-rewrite-study/), the fewest at 51 to 60 characters (39% to 42%) and 99.9% of those over 70. Google itself says there is no limit on title length and long ones are just cut off. Both can be true: a long title is allowed, but you lose control of what shows. Aim for about 60 characters, important words first.

Titles may matter for local rankings too. In Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey of local SEO practitioners, keywords in the title of the page your Business Profile links to rank [fourth among local organic factors](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/). That is opinion, not measurement.

**How to check yours.** Search Google for `site:yourbusiness.com.au` and read the blue links down the page. Look for pages called "Home", "Services" or just your business name, and for pages sharing a title.

**What good looks like.** Google asks for titles that are descriptive and concise, and says to avoid vague labels like "Home" and boilerplate text repeated on every page. Put your business name once, at the start or the end. For a Joondalup electrician:

| Page             | Weak title | Better title                                      |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Home             | Home       | Electrician in Joondalup - Your Business          |
| Switchboard page | Services   | Switchboard Upgrades in Joondalup - Your Business |
| Contact page     | Contact Us | Contact Your Business - Joondalup Electrician     |

## How should headings be set out on a page?

Headings break a page into sections. The main one, the H1, says what the page is about, and H2s mark the sections under it. Google is relaxed about the details. Its [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says it doesn't matter to Google Search if headings are out of order, and there is "no magical, ideal amount of headings". So don't worry about a site audit tool that flags two H1s.

What matters is that the main heading says what the page is, because Google may use it as your title link. In a 2022 Semrush split test on about 200 listing pages of a travel site, Google had been rewriting the titles using the H1, and [matching the H1 to the written title gave 12% more organic clicks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-split-test-result-matching-the-h1-with-the-title-tag/). In an earlier Semrush test on 335 category pages of a Dutch online shop, [promoting headings that were really sections of the whole page from H3 to H2 gave 4.5% more clicks](https://www.semrush.com/blog/seo-split-test-result-how-to-use-headings-ffectively/) over 21 days. Each test was on one large site, not a trade business, so read them as a direction, not a promise.

**How to check yours.** Open a service page and read the first big line of text. If it says "Welcome" or "Quality you can trust", it isn't helping. To see which line is the H1, press Ctrl+U to view the page source and search for `<h1`.

**What good looks like.** The H1 says the same thing as the title in plain words, such as "Switchboard upgrades in Joondalup and the northern suburbs". The H2s are the questions a customer asks: what it costs, how long it takes, which suburbs you cover.

## Why does each service need its own page?

A page has only one title and one main heading. A single Services page listing ten jobs can name only one of them in its title, so it competes for none of them well. A page for each service can be titled and written for exactly what someone searches.

Whitespark's 2026 survey ranks a [dedicated page for each service](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) first among all local organic factors. Again, that is the practitioners' view rather than a test.

Pages for the suburbs you serve are where owners get into trouble. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) name "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" and "substantially similar pages" as doorway abuse. A suburb page that is your service page with the suburb name swapped in is exactly that. Sterling Sky, a local SEO agency, advises making each area page [as unique as possible](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/how-to-create-unique-and-helpful-service-area-pages-for-local-businesses/), with local detail and photos from the job, and warns that thin service area pages can bring a penalty from Google.

**How to check yours.** Write down the services you most want calls for, and check each has its own page with its own title and heading. If you have suburb pages, read two side by side. If only the suburb name changes, they are doing you no good.

**What good looks like.** A Bayswater plumber has a page each for blocked drains, hot water systems and gas fitting. Each says what the job involves, a rough price, how quickly they can come out and the suburbs they cover, with photos of real jobs. A suburb page exists only where they do a lot of work and can show it: jobs done there and reviews from local customers.

## How do I set up a Google Business Profile properly?

Map results come from your Business Profile, and Whitespark's 2026 survey ranks the [primary category](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) as the top factor for showing in them. Verify the profile first: Google says that makes a business [more likely to show up in search results](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). Then get these right.

- **The primary category.** Google says to [select a primary category that best describes your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en) and to pick a specific one, such as "Nail salon" rather than "Salon". Add a few extra categories for your main services, but Google says not to select one for every product or service.
- **The real business name.** Google says your name [should reflect your business's real-world name](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), and that adding unnecessary information to it could get the profile suspended. Practitioners in Whitespark's survey still rank keywords in the name third for the map pack, so it may work, but it breaks Google's rules and risks the profile. Use your real name.
- **Every detail filled in.** Google says businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local results. That means hours, services, a description of what you do and where, and, if you go to customers rather than them coming to you, a service area with your address hidden, as [Google's guidelines ask](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).
- **Your own photos.** Google describes photos as a way to show customers what you offer. In a 2021 test, Sterling Sky added photos to four listings and saw [no measurable impact to rankings](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/photos-ranking-google-my-business/), so treat them as something for customers: your work, your van and your team, not stock images.
- **Reviews, asked for and answered.** Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity, that how well known a business is depends partly on how many reviews it has, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US consumers found [97% read reviews for local businesses](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) and 80% are likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews. Google suggests [sending customers a review link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en), replying promptly, by name and briefly, and bans offering discounts or other incentives for reviews.
- **The same name, address and phone as your website.** Google builds profiles partly from [information on a business's official website](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2721884?hl=en), so the two should agree. Whether mismatches elsewhere hurt rankings is disputed: when Sterling Sky let a paid listing service lapse and a wrong name reappeared, it [didn't have any measurable negative SEO impact](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/local-data-aggregators/). Match your details because customers use them to call you.

**How to check yours.** Search your business name on Google Maps on your phone. Check the category, hours, phone number and photos, then compare them line by line with your website's footer and contact page. ## Why are these cheapest to fix when the site is built?

Titles, headings and a page for each service are decided when a site is planned, and getting them right then costs very little. Adding them to a site built without them means new pages, new menus and rewritten copy: a second job. ## What SEO can wait?

Some things owners get sold early matter much less.

- **Regular Business Profile posts.** In 2021 Sterling Sky posted weekly for nine weeks on three listings, tracking 441 keywords each, and found [no measurable impact on rankings](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/do-google-posts-impact-ranking/) in the local pack. Whitespark's survey ranks the number of posts near the bottom. Post if customers read them, not for rankings.
- **Meta keywords.** Google says [it doesn't use the keywords meta tag](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).
- **A set word count.** Google says the length of content alone doesn't matter for ranking. Write as much as the customer needs.
- **A keyword-stuffed domain name.** Google says keywords in a domain name have hardly any effect beyond appearing in breadcrumbs.
- **Yearly directory listing packages.** Sterling Sky's view is that if your main goal is Google rankings, it's likely you don't need one.

## The checklist

1. Every page has its own title of about 60 characters, naming the service and the place, with your business name once.
2. Each page's main heading says the same thing as its title, and H2s mark real sections.
3. Every service you want calls for has its own page.
4. Suburb pages exist only where you have real jobs, photos or reviews from that suburb.
5. Your Business Profile is verified, under your real name, with the most specific primary category that fits.
6. Hours, services, service area and description are filled in, and the photos are your own.
7. You ask every customer for a review, and you reply.
8. Your name, address and phone number match exactly on your website and your profile.
