# How much does a website cost in Perth?

Perth studios publish website prices from a few hundred dollars to well over $20,000, and most professionally built small business sites they describe land somewhere between $2,000 and $10,000. Where yours falls depends on what kind of site it is, how much of it is designed for you and who builds it. This guide sets out the published ranges for each kind of site, what keeps costing after launch, and how to compare two quotes. If you are weighing up WordPress in particular, our [WordPress website cost guide](/wordpress-website-cost-perth) goes into its hosting, plugins and upkeep in detail.

## Why two quotes for the same site can be thousands apart

Usually because they are not quotes for the same site. CLIK puts it simply: "you are rarely comparing the same thing." The differences tend to come from five places.

- **Template or custom design.** A theme with your logo and photos dropped in is a few days' work. A layout designed around how your customers buy is a few weeks'.
- **Who writes the words.** Many quotes assume you supply the text. The Web Shop puts professional copywriting at $500 to $2,000 on top.
- **What the site has to do.** A site that explains your business is one job. A site that takes bookings, payments or orders, or sends enquiries into the software you already use, is a bigger one. [Web Art Design](https://webartdesign.com.au/blog/how-much-does-custom-website-cost-australia/) puts moderate integrations, such as payments or email marketing, at $500 to $2,000 extra and complex ones, such as a CRM or accounting sync, at $3,000 or more.
- **Search groundwork.** Some builds plan pages around what people search for; others leave it for later. RockingWeb lists SEO setup at $1,000 to $3,000 when it is priced separately.
- **Who builds it.** Web Art Design puts Perth metro rates at $80 to $120 an hour. An agency's rate also carries its account managers and its office.

Neither end is wrong in itself. [JetBlok](https://jetblok.com.au/blog/website-design-cost-perth-first-year-2026) makes the fair point that a five-page site for a one-van trade business doesn't need a $10,000 build, and a multi-location retailer doesn't need a $999 one. Match the budget to the job the site has to do.

## What a website keeps costing after launch

The build is the first line of the bill, not the last. These are the running costs to budget for, as the studios above published them on 4 November 2026.

- **Your domain.** JetBlok quotes VentraIP at $9.95 for the first year of a new .com.au and $22.95 a year to renew. Register it in your business's name, not your developer's.
- **Hosting.** The Web Shop puts hosting at $100 to $500 a year. Web Art Design puts shared hosting at $5 to $15 a month and managed WordPress hosting at $30 to $75 a month.
- **Maintenance.** Web Art Design puts basic updates and backups at $120 to $200 a month; RockingWeb puts maintenance at $100 to $500 a month. A WordPress site needs more of this than most, because of its plugins; the [WordPress cost guide](/wordpress-website-cost-perth) explains why.
- **Changes.** New pages, new photos and price changes are either included in a care plan or charged by the hour.

On our builds, running costs charged by other providers, such as hosting and your domain, are paid to them directly, and our terms list them before you commit. There is no plugin layer to keep updated.

## How much does a website cost in Australia?

Much the same as in Perth. Web Art Design's Australian guide, published in February 2026, puts a basic brochure website of 5 to 10 pages at $3,500 to $5,500, a small business website of 10 to 15 pages at $7,500 to $12,000, a basic online shop at $8,500 to $18,000, and a custom site with advanced features at $15,000 to $25,000 or more.

## Is a cheap website worth it?

It can be. A one-page site that says what you do, where you work and how to get in touch is enough for plenty of sole traders, and studios publish starter packages well under $2,000 for exactly that. The Web Shop's starts at $297 for one page and JetBlok's Starter is $999 for up to five pages.

What makes a cheap site expensive is what it leaves out: no planning around what customers search for, an enquiry form nobody tested, a domain registered in someone else's name, or a template you cannot change without the person who set it up. Ask about those, not the price, and a low quote can be a good buy.

## What should I ask before I sign?

1. Is the design custom, or a template with our content added?
2. Who writes the page content, and is it planned around what people search for?
3. What is the total yearly running cost, line by line?
4. Who owns the domain, the hosting account and the website files?
5. What does a change cost after launch, and who makes it?
