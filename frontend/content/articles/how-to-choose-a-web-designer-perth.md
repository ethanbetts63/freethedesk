# How do I choose a web designer in Perth?

Most owners buy a website rarely, so few have much practice. The quotes look alike, and the differences show up after launch: when you want a change, when the domain renewal goes to someone else's inbox, or when you try to leave. This guide gets those differences into the open before you sign, whoever you hire.

## What to decide before you ask for quotes

business.gov.au says [the first step in setting up a business website](https://business.gov.au/online-and-digital/business-website/set-up-a-business-website) is to "decide what you want it to achieve." Write these down. That page is your brief.

- **What the site has to do.** Bring in calls, take bookings, sell products, or show you are real when someone checks you out. Taking payments is a bigger job than explaining your services.
- **Which pages it needs.** Usually home, a page for each main service, about and contact. A quote for "a five-page site" means little until you know which five.
- **Who writes the words and supplies the photos.** Squarespace's guide to [working with a web designer](https://www.squarespace.com/blog/work-with-web-designer) asks: "Who's writing the copy? Who's sourcing images? And when are the assets due?" Many quotes assume you write the text.
- **Your budget range.** Our guide to [what a website costs in Perth](/website-cost-perth) sets out the ranges Perth studios publish, and the running costs after launch.

The WA Small Business Development Corporation (SBDC) suggests using a written brief to [get three to five quotes](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/templates-tools-guides/guides/choosing-a-website-professional). Quotes against the same brief can be compared. If you are still weighing up a DIY builder, read [website builder or web designer](/website-builder-vs-web-designer-perth) first.

## Freelancer, small studio or agency?

All three build good and bad sites. What differs is who does the work and who covers when something goes wrong.

| Who          | Good at                                                                        | Watch for                                                 |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Freelancer   | Lower price, and you deal with the person doing the work                       | One person's skills and availability                      |
| Small studio | Direct contact with the builders, and someone to cover if one is away          | Whether the work is passed to subcontractors              |
| Agency       | Design, development, copy and marketing under one roof, with a project manager | Higher prices, and a manager between you and the builders |

Clutch's guide to [hiring a freelancer or an agency](https://clutch.co/resources/when-to-hire-a-freelance-web-designer-vs-agency) puts it this way: a freelancer "is more affordable", but the site "will be limited to the ideas and skill sets of the freelancer", while an agency has "more stability and structure" and you will "most likely spend thousands more."

## How to read a web designer's portfolio

- **Ask for live links.** Rise Web Design, a Perth studio, says in its [guide to choosing a Perth web designer](https://risewebdesign.com.au/how-to-choose-a-web-designer-in-perth-and-avoid-the-dodgy-ones/) that a portfolio "should be live, clickable websites, not just screenshots."
- **Open them on your phone.** The SBDC says to [check how the site appears on mobile devices](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/templates-tools-guides/guides/choosing-a-website-professional). Tap the phone number, open the menu, start filling in the contact form.
- **Test the speed.** Paste each address into Google's free [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and read the mobile result. Google's documentation says [a score of 90 or above is considered good](https://developers.google.com/speed/docs/insights/v5/about) and below 50 is poor. One slow site proves little; a portfolio of sites all below 50 is worth asking about.
- **Search for what those businesses do.** Type a few clients' service and suburb into Google. One missing site proves nothing, but if none appear for anything, ask why.
- **Ring a past client.** The SBDC suggests asking whether a studio [will give you details of existing clients to contact](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/templates-tools-guides/guides/choosing-a-website-professional). Ask that owner whether it launched on time and on budget, what happened when they wanted a change, and whether they have their own logins.

## Who owns the website when it is finished?

A website is several things, and each has an owner.

### The domain name

You do not buy a .com.au name outright. auDA, which runs the .au domain, says in its [glossary](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/learn-about-au-domain-names/glossary-of-domain-name-terms/) that the registrant is "the individual or organisation who holds a domain name licence." Registrants don't own the name; they hold a licence for a set period, and a lapsed name can be registered by someone else.

auDA's page on [com.au domain names](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/the-different-au-domain-names/com-au-domain-names/) says they are for businesses with a connection to Australia, that "the most common way to qualify is by having an active ABN or ACN," and that the name needs a connection to your business. Someone else can register one for you with your explicit written permission, but "the details provided determine who holds the domain name licence," so they should be yours, and your own contact details mean you know when to renew. auDA's [licensing rules](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/au-rules-and-policies/au-domain-administration-rules-licensing-2/) say anyone who registers a name on your behalf must make sure you are recorded as the registrant. A wrong registrant can be corrected only after a genuine error, and only within 14 days of registration, so check the details as soon as the name is registered.

### The hosting account

The safest set-up is a hosting account in your business's name, with a login for the studio. If the studio hosts the site, get in writing how it moves to you if you leave. The SBDC suggests [agreeing at the start](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/blog/7-things-do-you-sign-new-website) "how the agreement will end, including how access, logins and passwords will be transferred to you."

### The site files, words and photos

The SBDC suggests asking whether a studio provides [full ownership and backup copies of the code files and images](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/templates-tools-guides/guides/choosing-a-website-professional), and having the contract confirm you own the copyright to the entire website, except for any stock photos or graphics. Ask whether any stock photo licence passes to you.

### Your Google accounts

- **Google Business Profile.** Google says [a profile can have multiple owners but only one primary owner](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100). Be the primary owner. A studio added as a manager can still do the work, though Google says managers can't add or remove users or remove the profile.
- **Search Console.** An owner [has full control over properties in Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7687615), and Google says that if all verified owners are removed, the remaining users lose access after a grace period. Be a verified owner, not just a user the studio added.
- **Google Analytics.** The [Administrator role](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9305587) has full control of Analytics and can manage users. That should be you.

## What should a web design quote and contract include?

Get these in writing before you pay anything.

- **Scope.** The pages, the features, and who writes and supplies what.
- **What counts as a change.** How many rounds of changes are included and what extra ones cost. The SBDC adds that [fixing bugs such as broken links or mobile display issues](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/blog/7-things-do-you-sign-new-website) shouldn't be charged as extras.
- **Whether the price is fixed.** The SBDC suggests asking for a fixed price quote, with the studio bearing any extra cost if the job takes more hours than it estimated, unless the scope changes and you agree to pay more.
- **Payment stages.** The SBDC advises against paying in full upfront: a deposit, then payments as the project reaches set milestones.
- **Timeline.** The launch date, what the studio needs from you by when, and what happens if it runs late.
- **After launch.** Who fixes faults, for how long, and at what cost.
- **Running costs.** Domain, hosting, licences and any monthly fee, line by line.

### What Australian law says

This is general information, not legal advice, from the ACCC's pages as we read them in November 2026. The ACCC says a service bought for a business is [covered by consumer guarantees](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/small-business/small-business-toolkit/rights-and-responsibilities-of-your-small-business/small-business-rights-and-responsibilities) if it meets at least one condition, one being that it "costs less than $100,000 including GST." Services must be provided with due care and skill, be fit for purpose and, where no time frame was agreed, be provided within a reasonable time.

Pre-written terms a business gives every customer, which you can take or leave, are what the ACCC calls a standard form contract. Since 9 November 2023, it says, proposing, using or relying on [unfair contract terms](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/contracts) in one is banned, and penalties apply. Small businesses with fewer than 100 employees, or less than $10 million in annual turnover, are covered for contracts made or varied since then. Its examples include terms that let one party, but not the other, change or end the contract. If a contract worries you, get legal advice.

## Red flags when hiring a web designer

- **Guaranteed number-one rankings.** Google's page [Do you need an SEO?](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) says "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google," and if someone guarantees first place, "find someone else." Our guide to [small business SEO in Perth](/small-business-seo-perth) covers what does work.
- **The domain registered in the studio's name.** auDA's rules say your business must be recorded as the registrant, even when someone else registers the name for you. A name held by the studio leaves your renewals, and your email, in someone else's hands.
- **No live examples.** The SBDC says to "be wary of companies who are reluctant to provide you with details of their experience."
- **A price with no scope.** It cannot be compared, and the extras turn up later.
- **Lock-in to a system you cannot take with you.** The SBDC says to [be cautious if a developer's contract states that they retain ownership](https://www.smallbusiness.wa.gov.au/blog/7-things-do-you-sign-new-website) of your website or domain, and to avoid agencies that require lock-in contracts. If the site runs on the studio's own platform, ask what you get if you leave: files another developer can host, or only your text. Moving a site without losing rankings is a [job of its own](/website-migration-seo-perth).
- **A monthly fee with no list of what it buys.** "Maintenance" can mean updates, backups, fixes and changes, or only updates. On WordPress it matters more, as our [WordPress cost guide](/wordpress-website-cost-perth) explains.

## Questions to ask every web designer

Ask each studio the same questions, in writing, so the answers line up side by side.

1. Can I see three live sites you built in the last two years, and ring one of those owners?
2. Who will do the work: you, your staff, or subcontractors?
3. What does the price include: which pages and features, who writes the words, how many rounds of changes?
4. Is the price fixed, and what happens if the job takes longer than you expect?
5. What are the payment stages and the launch date?
6. Will my business be the domain's registrant, with my contact details?
7. Whose name is the hosting account in, and who pays for it?
8. Will I be primary owner of my Business Profile, a verified owner in Search Console and an administrator in Analytics?
9. Will I get the site files, in a form another developer could host and work on?
10. What does a change cost after launch, and what is in any monthly fee?
11. What is the total yearly running cost, line by line?

A good studio will answer all eleven without fuss. If you already have a site and are not sure it needs replacing, an [SEO audit](/seo) will show what is holding it back.
