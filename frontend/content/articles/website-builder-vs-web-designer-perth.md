# Should I use a website builder or a web designer in Perth?

If you are just starting out, offer a handful of services and like doing things yourself, a website builder such as Wix or Squarespace is often the right answer, and plenty of Perth businesses run well on one. Paying someone to build it starts to make sense once the site has to bring in work from Google, load fast on a phone and stay yours if you ever move it. This guide compares the four usual routes on cost, time, ownership and room to grow, sets out what the speed data and Google actually say, and ends with a short list of which to choose for your situation.

## When a website builder is the right call

A builder is a sound choice when:

- **You are a new sole trader.** You need to be findable and look credible now, and a few hundred dollars a year is easier to justify than a build.
- **The site is a simple brochure.** What you do, where you work, some photos and how to get in touch.
- **The budget is tight.** No upfront build, and you can cancel if the business changes direction.
- **You like doing it yourself.** You will change prices, photos and opening hours more often than anyone else would.

The big publishers say much the same. Search Engine Journal's [Wix vs. WordPress comparison](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/wix-vs-wordpress-seo/414144/), published in January 2025, concludes that "for small businesses and online stores, Wix might be exactly what you need." Semrush's [comparison](https://www.semrush.com/blog/wix-vs-wordpress/) calls Wix "a good option for beginners who want to build a static website or a simple online presence without any coding."

### What to get right if you build it yourself

- **Your own domain, in your own name.** Not a free subdomain, and not registered by a friend. [Wix's help centre](https://support.wix.com/en/article/exporting-or-embedding-your-wix-site-elsewhere) says you can buy and manage the domain with another provider and connect it, though connecting one needs a paid plan. A domain you hold yourself goes with you if you ever leave.
- **A page for each service.** One page that lists everything you do gives Google one page to rank for all of it.
- **Proper page titles.** Each page's title should say the service and the place, in the words customers type.
- **A Google Business Profile** that matches the site's name, address and phone number.

## Are website builders bad for SEO?

Not in themselves. In Google's [May 2023 SEO office hours](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/may), Martin Splitt was asked whether it matters if a site uses WordPress or a custom-made CMS. His answer: "No, it generally doesn't matter. However, the performance and behavior of it does though." John Mueller said the same in a 2022 Ask Googlebot video, reported by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/does-the-cms-matter-for-search-rankings/457580/): "all mainstream CMS systems can create pages that work well in search."

Where publishers differ is on control and speed. Semrush says WordPress "generally provides you with more control over your site's SEO." Search Engine Journal's comparison lists the main SEO drawbacks of Wix as "code bloat," limits on how much you can change your site's structure, and moving elsewhere being "a headache." The measured data below is kinder to the builders on speed than that, but both publishers agree a builder covers the basics for a small site.

## How fast are Wix, Squarespace, Shopify and WordPress sites?

Google's [Core Web Vitals documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) says it highly recommends site owners "achieve good Core Web Vitals for success with Search." These measures cover how fast the main content loads, how quickly the page responds to a tap and how much it jumps around while loading.

HTTP Archive's [Core Web Vitals Technology Report](https://httparchive.org/reports/techreport/tech?tech=WordPress,Wix,Squarespace,Shopify) shows what share of sites on each platform pass all three, measured from real Chrome visits. In its October 2026 data, on mobile:

| Platform    | Sites passing Core Web Vitals, worldwide | Sites passing, Australia |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Wix         | 81%                                      | 86%                      |
| Shopify     | 77%                                      | 78%                      |
| Squarespace | 71%                                      | 53%                      |
| WordPress   | 49%                                      | 52%                      |

These figures move every month, so treat them as a snapshot, and the Australian ones rest on far fewer sites: about 2,700 on Wix and 3,900 on Squarespace, against about 42,000 on WordPress. The pattern matches the [Web Almanac's 2025 CMS chapter](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/cms), which found that "vertically integrated platforms tend to deliver more consistent Core Web Vitals, especially on mobile," while self-hosted systems vary much more with their themes, plugins and add-ons. It adds that "implementation decisions often matter more than platform defaults." A builder site stuffed with large photos and apps can still fail, and a well-built site on any platform can pass.

So speed is not usually where a builder lets a small business down. Control and leaving are.

## Where website builders run out

**How much you can change.** A builder gives you its layouts, its settings and its app store. Wix's help centre says that "it's not possible to [switch to a different template](https://support.wix.com/en/article/switching-your-site-template) for a site you already created"; you start a new site and copy your pages across. Something the platform doesn't offer, such as a quote form that uses your own pricing or a page structure built around how your customers search, may not be possible at all.

**Leaving.** This is the part to understand before you start. Wix's help centre says that "since Wix is a SaaS solution, your site must run on Wix's servers." The same page says the content you build on Wix belongs to you, but you [can't use another host](https://support.wix.com/en/article/exporting-or-embedding-your-wix-site-elsewhere) for the site itself. Squarespace lets you [export some content](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Export-your-site-content) as a file made for importing into WordPress. That covers layout pages, one blog with its posts, text blocks and image blocks. It does not cover store pages, portfolio pages, product blocks, video blocks, drafts, style settings or custom CSS, and Squarespace warns that images might not come across into a self-hosted WordPress site. Shopify lets you [export your products](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/import-export/export-products) to a spreadsheet file. In practice, moving off a builder means rebuilding the site and redirecting the old addresses.

**Growing past a few pages.** A site that has to win work from search tends to grow: a page per service, then per suburb, then guides and past jobs. Builders can hold those pages, but every structural change is limited to what the platform allows.

## Where WordPress fits

WordPress sits between the two. W3Techs puts it on [38.5% of all websites](https://w3techs.com/technologies/overview/content_management), 57.4% of those using a content management system it tracks, as of 22 November 2026. Because so many developers know it, you can usually change who looks after it, and its plugins cover most things a small business needs.

The cost is upkeep. Every plugin and theme needs updating, someone has to test the site after each round, and the speed figures above suggest it needs more care than the builders to stay fast. ## What is a custom-coded website?

A custom-coded site is written for your business, without a builder or a general-purpose platform underneath. Each page loads only what it needs, the structure follows how your customers search, and nothing is limited to a plugin or an app store.

It suits a business whose site has to bring in a steady flow of enquiries from search, needs something specific such as bookings that follow its own rules, or has outgrown a builder. It does not suit everyone. It costs more to start than a builder plan. Structural changes, such as a new section or a new feature, go through a developer, so make sure the code is handed over in a form another developer could pick up. And for a one-page site that just says who you are and how to call, it is more than you need.

## Which one should I choose?

- **Just starting, one or two services, small budget:** a builder. Squarespace or Wix, with your own domain.
- **Selling products online yourself:** Shopify.
- **Publishing articles every week, with several people editing:** WordPress, with someone keeping it updated.
- **Already on a builder and it brings in enough work:** stay, and fix the page titles and service pages first.
- **The site needs to win work from Google, be fast on a phone and be yours to move:** a custom build, or a well-built WordPress site if you are happy to pay for upkeep.
