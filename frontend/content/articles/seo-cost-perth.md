# How much does SEO cost in Perth?

Most SEO in Perth is sold as a monthly retainer, and the prices Perth firms publish for one run from about $500 to more than $10,000 a month. A retainer is not the only way to pay, though. You can buy a one-off audit, which tells you what to fix and leaves the doing to you or whoever looks after your site, or you can pay Google for clicks while your rankings catch up. This guide sets out what each costs, what it buys and when it makes sense, using the prices Perth firms publish themselves.

## Three ways Perth businesses pay for SEO

Every price in this section is as the firm published it on its own page when we read it on 4 November 2026. Some firms quote before tax and some after, so check which before you set two quotes side by side.

### A monthly retainer

A retainer pays an agency or freelancer to work on your site every month: fixing technical problems, writing and improving pages, earning links and reporting what moved. It is how most SEO is sold, and the published prices vary more than anything else here.

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Loudachris](https://www.loudachris.com.au/seo/cost)                                             | Freelancers about $500 to $1,200, specialist agencies about $1,500 to $4,000, big-brand agencies $4,000 to $10,000 or more                                           |
| [Perth Digital Edge](https://perthdigitaledge.com.au/blog/seo-pricing/)                          | $500 to $5,000, with high-end work starting at $5,000                                                                                                                |
| [Loaded Communications](https://loadedcommunications.com.au/how-much-does-seo-cost-in-perth/)    | $1,500 to $3,000 for a small business, $2,500 to $5,000 for a medium one, $5,000 to $8,000 or more for a large one                                                   |
| [All Marketing](https://www.allmarketing.com.au/blog/seo-cost-australia-perth-business-pricing/) | $1,000 to $2,000 as the small business entry point, $2,500 to $5,000 for competitive Perth work, $6,000 to $12,000 for multi-location, online shops or national work |
| [SEO Perth Expert](https://seoperth.expert/perth-seo-prices/)                                    | Its own plans, $970 to $3,300                                                                                                                                        |

A retainer makes sense when there is steady work to do: a competitive market, a site that needs new pages every month, or links that have to be earned over time. The firms that sell retainers make a fair point against one-off work, too. Loudachris says that without ongoing work after a one-off project, rankings typically slip within about three to six months as competitors keep moving. What a retainer cannot tell you, before you sign, is whether the monthly work is aimed at the right things.

### Paid clicks

Google Ads puts you at the top of the results today, for as long as you pay. There are two costs: the clicks, which you pay Google for, and a management fee if someone runs the account for you.

What a click costs depends on the search. On a roof replacement campaign and a gutter replacement campaign that [Loudachris published figures for](https://www.loudachris.com.au/google-ads/cost), a click cost $13.16 and $18.72 on average over the 30 days to 17 July 2026. The same guide found Australian agencies publishing entry management packages from about $1,000 to $2,500 a month when it checked their pricing pages in October 2026.

Ads are useful for testing which searches bring enquiries and for filling the gap while your rankings grow. The catch is that every click is rented: the day the budget stops, so do the visits.

### A one-off audit

An audit is a paid review of your site and how it shows up on Google, ending in a written list of what to fix. You pay once. You, your IT person or your developer make the changes.

- **Technical audits.** All Marketing puts a technical audit at roughly $1,500 to $5,000.
- **Project work.** Perth Digital Edge and Loudachris both put one-off projects, audits and migrations among them, at $2,000 to $10,000 depending on scope.
- **By the hour.** All Marketing puts the typical Perth agency rate at about $220 an hour. Loudachris puts Australian SEO consulting at about $100 to $250 an hour and lists audits among the jobs hourly work suits.

Many agencies also offer a free audit, including Perth Digital Edge, Loudachris and [TVDM](https://tvdm.au/pricing/). A free audit can be useful, and it is a fair way for an agency to find clients. Just know that it is written by the people who hope to do the work, and its next step is usually a monthly proposal.

An audit does nothing on its own. As All Marketing puts it, "an audit that identifies 46 errors is a starting point, not a deliverable". Its value is in the changes that get made from it, so a good one tells you which changes matter most and how to make them.

## Which one to pay for first

If you need enquiries this week, ads are the only one of the three that can deliver them, and a small, tightly targeted budget is a reasonable test.

If you already know what needs doing and have no time to do it, a retainer from someone who shows you what ships each month is a reasonable buy.

If you are not sure what is holding your site back, or you have been quoted a retainer and can't tell whether it is worth it, start with an audit. For about the price of one month of the cheapest retainers, you find out what is wrong and what to fix first. Then you can make the changes yourself, hand the list to your developer, or ask an agency to quote against it. A retainer bought after an audit is one you can hold to account.

## How much does SEO cost in Australia?

About the same as in Perth. Loudachris puts specialist agency SEO for Australian small and medium businesses at around $1,500 to $4,000 a month in 2026, and a full-time in-house SEO hire at around $80,000 to $120,000 a year in salary, plus tools. All Marketing's answer for Australia is "somewhere between $1,000 and $12,000 a month depending on your market". Both were read on 4 November 2026. Several of the Perth ranges above are national figures too, which is a fair sign that what you pay depends more on how competitive your market is than on which city you are in.

## Why do SEO retainers vary so much?

Because a retainer is mostly hours, and both the hours and the rate vary.

All Marketing works it through: at the typical Perth agency rate of about $220 an hour, a $3,000 retainer buys about 13.6 hours of work a month. In one example it gives, roughly five of a client's 12.7 monthly hours went on reporting, calls and account administration. Convert any quote into hours and ask how many of them go on the work itself.

The rest of the spread comes from what the work involves. A single-location trade in a quiet suburb needs far less than an online shop competing nationally. Link building, regular new content and technical fixes on a large site all add hours. All Marketing also makes the uncomfortable point that some proposals are priced from what the agency thinks the buyer can afford, rather than from what the work needs.

## What should I ask before paying for SEO?

Whoever you pay, and however you pay them, get answers to these first:

1. **What exactly will you do, and how will I see it was done?** Pages changed, content published and fixes made should be listed, not summarised.
2. **How many hours does the fee buy, and at what rate?** This is the fairest way to compare two quotes that look nothing alike.
3. **Is there a lock-in contract?** If there is, ask what you get if you leave early.
4. **Who owns the accounts?** Search Console, Analytics, your Business Profile and your Ads account should all be in your business's name, with the agency added as a user.
5. **What access do you need?** [Google's own advice on hiring an SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) is to give read access to Search Console for an audit, not write access.
6. **Do you guarantee rankings?** Google says plainly that "no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google". Anyone who promises one is a reason to keep looking.
