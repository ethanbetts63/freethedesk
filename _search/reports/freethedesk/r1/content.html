This is a baseline, not a performance review

Search Console has twenty-two days of history for this property, beginning 29 August 2026: 26 impressions and 4 clicks. No window in that is long enough to support a percentage change, so nothing here is a trend. What follows is the starting position, precise enough to be measured against later, plus one concrete fault found by checking the site rather than the data.

All four clicks went to the homepage, which sat at position 1.3 across 10 impressions — a 40% click-through rate. That is the signature of people searching the business by name and finding it immediately. Every other page on the site earned impressions and no clicks.

What twenty-six impressions can and cannot support

A single impression moves any rate on this page by several points. Positions drawn from three or four impressions describe those three or four searches and nothing wider. Treat every figure in this report as a recorded starting value, not as an estimate of how the site performs.

The page ranking for the commercial terms is a 404, and Google does not know yet

/website-development-perth drew 15 impressions in the period, more than any page on the site including the homepage, at an average position of 28.9. It is the page that answers "perth website development", "custom web development perth" and "custom web design perth" — the terms the web development side of the business is sold on.

It returns HTTP 404. The URL serves the site's error template: an h1 reading "404", the line "This page could not be found", and — because the template inherits the site's default metadata — the homepage's title and meta description.

Google's view of the same URL, read through the URL Inspection API on 19 September, still reports it Submitted and indexed, last crawled successfully on 5 September 2026. Two things follow: the page broke on or after 5 September, and Google is still showing searchers a cached copy it believes is healthy — which is exactly what the 15 impressions are.

The deploy history settles when and why. A production deploy at 08:11 on 7 September renamed the page to /website-development — same content, de-localised — and updated the sitemap to the new path in the same commit. What it did not do is redirect the old URL. So the address Google knows, ranks and still shows to searchers has served the error template ever since; the break is not a removal or a regression but the missing half of a rename.

What this costs right now

Very little in clicks, because at position 28.9 almost nobody was reaching it anyway. The cost is ahead: when Google next recrawls, a 404 on a submitted URL drops it from the index, and the site loses its only ranking page for its highest-value commercial terms. The fix is worth making before that recrawl, not after.

The site's own pages appear together in the same results

Grouped by date, the property recorded 26 impressions; grouped by page, 63. On most properties the page tier is the smaller number, because Search Console withholds the URL for a large share of searches. Here it is 2.4 times larger, and that is reproducible against the API.

The arithmetic is simple: a date-level impression counts one appearance in one set of results; a page-level impression counts each URL shown. When two or three FreeTheDesk URLs surface in the same results — likely, on a nine-page site describing adjacent services in similar language — that is one site impression and several page impressions. Not a fault, but page-tier counts describe appearances, not searches, and the tiers must not be added or compared.

The query tier is the constraint on everything in the next section. Google named the search term for 13 of 26 impressions and none of the 4 clicks. Every query-level statement in this report covers that half.

The best-placed pages are the ones with nothing to sell

Ordering the nine ranked pages by position rather than by impressions inverts the site. /seo sits at position 2.0, /legal/privacy at 5.0, /licensing at 5.2 and /legal/dealer-subscription-terms at 7.0 — a subscription terms page on the first page of results. Meanwhile /dealership-website-builder, a product page, averages 14.6, and /automation 27.5.

The honest reading is that the well-placed pages match long, specific, low-competition phrasing — a legal document ranks seventh because barely anything competes for its exact words. That is not authority. But it is evidence the site is fully crawled and indexed, which for a three-week-old property is the thing that had to be true.

Where the named queries do reach the commercial terms, the positions are page three and four: "custom web design perth" at 20.0, "custom web development perth" at 31.0, "perth website development" at 37.5. One query — "custom web design perth" — is inside striking distance, and it is the single closest the site comes to competing for work.

Two of the six are for the dealer product and both sit past position 80. Neither "desking tool" nor "vehicle dealer website" has a page written against it: /dealership-website-builder is 455 words, the shortest page on the site and a third the length of /automation.

The portfolio page is competing in a client's own search results

"scooter shop fremantle" produced 2 impressions at position 43.5, served by /portfolio/scooter-shop — the case study for Scootershop, a separate property in this series. At position 43 it is harmless and it is not demand for FreeTheDesk. It is recorded because the failure mode it points at is real: a well-optimised case study can end up ranking above the client on the client's own name. Nothing suggests that is happening, and it is cheap to watch.

Nothing carries forward

This is the first report for the property. There is no inherited recommendation ledger, no running experiment and no previous figure to attribute a movement to. The live check recorded all nine URLs for the first time, so there is no site-change comparison either — what changed since the last check is, for now, everything.

Recommendations

  1. Redirect /website-development-perth to its renamed self

    id: fix-wdp-404 priority 1 cost: low

    The deploy history removes the ambiguity: the page was renamed to /website-development deliberately on 7 September and the sitemap was updated in the same deploy — the only missing piece is a permanent redirect from the old URL. One rule, shipped before the next recrawl, and the index standing transfers instead of dying stranded. Worth watching afterwards: the old, Perth-qualified URL held the site's only impressions for the Perth web-development terms, and whether the de-localised page keeps them is exactly what the experiment in section 08 reads. Evidence: section 02.

  2. Check nothing else was renamed without a redirect

    id: sitemap-404-audit priority 2 cost: low

    The current sitemap is clean — the 7 September deploy got it right — but the fault class is a rename that updates the sitemap and forgets the redirect, and that leaves no trace in the current sitemap at all. The URLs Google already knows are the exposure: check every URL Search Console has ever recorded an impression for still resolves, after any batch of renames. Nine pages today; cheap forever. Evidence: section 02.

  3. Give the 404 template its own title and metadata

    id: error-page-metadata priority 3 cost: low

    The error page currently inherits the homepage title and meta description, so a broken URL presents to a crawler as a legitimate page. That makes faults like the one above harder to spot from the outside and slower for Google to act on. A distinct title and a noindex directive on the error template make a 404 read as a 404.

  4. Build the dealer product pages out to match the service pages

    id: dealer-page-depth priority 4 cost: medium

    Both dealer-side queries sit past position 80, and /dealership-website-builder is 455 words against 1,661 for /seo and 1,050 for /automation. The two pages that rank respectably are the two with substance on them. This is the clearest lever the site has, and it is a content job rather than a technical one. Evidence: section 04.

  5. Leave the rest alone until there is data

    id: hold-for-data priority 5 cost: low

    Twenty-six impressions cannot distinguish a page that is underperforming from one that has not been seen. Optimising against these figures means optimising against noise, and it would also destroy the baseline this report exists to establish. The useful work over the next quarter is publishing and fixing, not tuning.

What was measured, and what this report cannot tell you

Measured. Every figure comes from the Google Search Console Search Analytics API for https://www.freethedesk.com.au/, web search, all countries, stored daily from 29 August 2026. Site totals use the date-only series, which is the complete one. Page and query figures are separate tiers and are labelled as such wherever they appear. All nine URLs the report describes were fetched and rendered with headless Chrome on 19 September 2026, and Google's own index view of the same nine was read through the URL Inspection API the same day.

No country filter. Twenty-four of twenty-six impressions are Australian; the remaining two are not excluded, because at this volume a filter removes rows without improving a figure.

Seasonality cannot be separated from cause. Twenty-two days of history, no prior year. Nothing in this report should be read as a trend, and no movement in it can be attributed to a season, a change on the site, or anything else.

Google named the query for half the impressions and none of the clicks. Section 04 describes 13 of 26 impressions. The other half were searches Google declined to disclose, and they may not resemble the ones it did.

The page tier overcounts here. Page-level impressions exceed site-level by 2.4 times because one search can show more than one of the site's URLs. Page impressions are appearances, not searches.

One render in the first pass was wrong, and the check was repeated. The first live pass recorded /licensing with no title and no body text. Fetched directly, the page serves 847 words and a correct title; the render had failed rather than the page being empty. The check was re-run and the corrected record is what this report and its stored baseline use. A rendered fetch is evidence, not proof, and a single failed render looks exactly like a broken page.

There is no analytics. Search Console carries no sessions, no conversions and no revenue, so nothing here can say what a click was worth or what happened after it. Every priority above is ranked on search evidence and commercial judgement, not on outcomes. Google Analytics was removed from this site and no replacement is collecting; restoring it is the single change that would most improve the next report.

The 404's origin is established. The live check found the URL returning 404; the deploy history (Vercel, read 20 September, after the period closed) shows why: a 7 September deploy renamed the page and updated the sitemap without redirecting the old URL. What deploy evidence cannot show is whether an earlier rename did the same thing to a URL that never earned impressions — recommendation 2 covers that.
