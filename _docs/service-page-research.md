# Service page research: AI SEO and website migration SEO

Read 2026-11-16, to model two new service pages on what ranks. Rivals were
chosen by where they rank, not by lists. One reading of each search on
google.com.au with `pws=0`, from Perth, in the analytics@ Chrome. Searches
naming Melbourne or Sydney were still run from Perth, so treat those results
as a guide, not as what a local searcher sees.

## Why only two pages

A new site should start with fewer, stronger pages and add narrower ones once
it is strong in its main area (John Mueller, 2020 and 2021). Google also
scores quality mostly at site level. Both topics sit close to what freethedesk
already sells. The evidence is in claim C16 of
`../ftp_search/_search/research/seo-studies.md`.

What the results say:

- Every search showed an AI Overview.
- For AI SEO, a site whose domain is the search itself ranks first in all three
  cities. Best-of lists take two or three of the nine places, so searchers are
  comparing agencies. Net Search is not in the Perth top nine.
- For migration, a search naming a city returns service pages. The search with
  no city returns checklists. A downloadable checklist could serve both.

## AI SEO pages

- **seoperth.expert (#1 Perth).** About 1,200 words, laid out as
  question-style headings. Weak: typos, side stories, no prices, no client
  proof. The Perth bar is low.
- **aiseoperth.net.au (#2 Perth).** Not a service page but a research site, run
  by a firm that sells the service. It publishes Perth data: 200 questions run
  3 times (600 answers), with 57.6% of AI Overview citations matching a URL
  already in the top 10. It offers a CSV download and a versioned method, and
  labels each finding as documented, inferred or hypothesis. Original data
  ranks.
- **seomelbourne.com (#1 Melbourne).** A free AI visibility check (two runs,
  then an email gate) at the top. Prices are published: audit from $1,000,
  monthly $1,000–4,000, a self-serve crawl tool at $18 a month. It has a named
  method, three client case studies with numbers, and a public live citation
  count (528 of 1,527 searches). It says AI SEO sits on top of SEO, not instead
  of it.
- **studiohawk.com.au (#3 Melbourne).** National agency. Big-brand logos and
  case studies, free audit, no prices, market statistics without sources.
- **aiseosydney.au (#1 Sydney).** Pitched at small business ("was for big
  business, until now"). Tiered prices, a $1,000 AI audit, a "why us instead
  of the other guys" table, and plain caveats. No case studies.
- **sydneydigitalmarketing.com.au (#2 Sydney).** A named tool, a 12-question
  FAQ and a free audit. No prices.

Questions they share: what AI SEO is and how it differs from SEO; whether it
replaces SEO (all say no); how to show up in ChatGPT and AI Overviews; how long
it takes; what it costs; whether you need llms.txt.

Where freethedesk can differ:

- **Prices.** Most rivals hide them; freethedesk's audits are priced up front.
- **llms.txt, sold plainly.** freethedesk sells it too (owner, 2026-11-16).
  Most rivals sell it as a ranking lever. Google says its AI features need no
  special files, and the ledger (T5) finds no ranking effect. So sell it for
  what it is, a clean summary of the site for AI tools to read, and never
  promise rankings or citations from it.
- **Our own data, as aiseoperth.net.au does it.** The audits already read AI
  crawler access and Search Console's Generative AI report.

## Website migration pages

- **digitalhitmen.com.au (#1 Perth).** About 3,500 unique words. Seven steps,
  platforms covered, about 37 FAQs, a team section, one case study and 107
  Google reviews. No prices.
- **seo-agency-perth.au (#2 Perth).** About 1,100 words. Five steps, a "what to
  prepare" list, three customer scenarios. No proof.
- **sentius.com.au (#1 Melbourne).** About 700 words, with timelines by
  migration type. Its proof figures are still placeholders ("Xx", lorem
  ipsum), yet it ranks first.
- **waypointdigital.com.au (#2 Melbourne).** The best model:
  - It lists what the client gets: a benchmark report, crawl export, redirect
    map, on-page transfer sheet, QA report, a signed launch checklist and 90
    days of monitoring.
  - It gives timelines by site size (under 50 pages takes 4–6 weeks).
  - It has a downloadable checklist and three case studies with numbers.
  - It says a very new or low-traffic site may not need the full project.
- **studiohawk.com.au (#1 Sydney).** Four steps, ten case studies, ten FAQs. It
  admits migration "isn't an exact science". No prices.
- **optimising.com.au (Melbourne #5 and national #3).** Six steps, aimed at
  the developers who do the build. Big-name case studies, timelines, and help
  for a migration that has already gone wrong.

Steps they share: baseline, then crawl and URL inventory, then a redirect map,
then staging checks, then launch, then about 90 days of monitoring.

Questions they share: will we lose rankings; when to bring SEO in; how long it
takes; what access you need; whether you build the site; whether you can fix a
migration that has already dropped.

Where freethedesk can differ:

- **A fixed price.** None of the six publishes one.
- **The rescue job.** The audit already reads Search Console, so "your traffic
  dropped after launch" is something freethedesk can sell today.
- **Tie it to the redesign service.**
- **Published evidence.** The ledger's Sistrix studies show about 3 months to
  recrawl, harm from 302 redirects, and damage from changing domain and
  structure at once.
