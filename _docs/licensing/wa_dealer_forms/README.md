# WA prescribed motor vehicle dealer forms

Downloaded 21 Aug 2026 from Consumer Protection WA and the WA legislation site;
DVS forms added 26 Aug 2026. Reference copies for
`../dealer_direct_to_your_door.md`. **Check for newer versions before relying on
any of these** — Consumer Protection reissues them and the regulations
consolidation below is out of date in one known respect.

**Read `../online_licensing.md` first.** We hold a Dealer Online account, so the
DVS forms below are the fallback path rather than the working one: Dealer Online
licenses factory-new stock and transfers dealership-owned vehicles directly in
the DVS system, which removes VL17 and MR9B from the customer's experience
entirely. Keep them here for the days DOS is down or a transaction falls outside
it.

**Two different form families live here.** Everything except VL-E79 is Consumer
Protection paperwork governing the *sale*. VL-E79 is Transport (DVS) paperwork
governing the *licensing*, and it is a dealer-side declaration signed by an
authorised officer of the business under its dealer licence number — the
customer never fills it. MR9B is the customer-facing one, but only for used
stock.

**VL17 is the one that makes the no-visit model possible.** For a vehicle not
currently licensed in WA, the application:

> must be presented in person by the proposed licence holder **or an authorised
> representative of a Motor Vehicle Dealership** with proof of ownership.

So it must be lodged in person — but *we* can be the ones to lodge it. The
customer signs their declaration, we sign the Seller's Declaration, and an
authorised rep takes it to DVS.

**Confirmed 26 Aug 2026**, and not only on the form. DoT's *License a vehicle in
Western Australia* page says the same thing in its own voice — "In most cases a
motor vehicle dealer will license a new vehicle", and "you **or an authorised
dealership representative** must present the application in person, with proof
of ownership from the seller (a licensed dealership in this case)". So the
no-visit promise never rested on a single inferred clause. It is moot for us in
any case now that we are on Dealer Online.

Consequences worth designing for:

- **Proof of ownership must show "paid in full".** A receipt or contract of sale
  showing purchase price, date of sale, VIN/plate/engine number, and the
  seller's name and signature — and receipts and tax invoices "must show *paid
  in full* in order to be accepted". **The balance therefore has to be paid
  before we can licence the bike**, which is an operational reason for taking it
  before delivery rather than on delivery per cl 3.1 of the sale contract.
- **Immobiliser — resolved, we are exempt.** DoT's immobiliser page lists
  "mopeds and motorcycles" among vehicles that "aren't required to have an
  immobiliser fitted". The requirement otherwise bites on the grant of a licence
  and on transfer, with the onus on the purchaser, but it does not reach our
  stock at all. Underlying rule: Road Traffic (Vehicles) Regulations 2014 Part 10
  r388; exemption gazetted in WA Government Gazette No. 205, 22 Nov 2002 p. 5563.
- **Related forms — now held.** VL12, VL186, VL50 and MR9 were downloaded
  26 Aug 2026 and are in the table above.
- Understating purchase price or dutiable value carries penalties up to $20,000,
  and false information up to 3 years imprisonment. The invoice we generate is a
  statutory document, not just a receipt.

**MR9B costs us paperwork, not the customer a trip. An earlier version of this
file said otherwise — corrected 26 Aug 2026:**

- It is **not valid unless signed by both the dealer and the purchaser**, and
  it is a physical carbon form — blue Seller's Copy, red Purchaser's Copy. A
  download-fill-upload loop does not produce a valid MR9B. This is the one real
  constraint, and it is entirely on our side of the counter.
- **We** must mail the blue copy to DVS **within 7 days of the sale** or risk a
  $100 infringement. We also forward or hand the red copy plus the current
  licence papers to the purchaser.
- **The purchaser has no lodgement deadline and no errand.** This file
  previously claimed they must submit the red copy within 14 days. That is
  wrong. The red copy is the purchaser's own record; DVS acts on our blue copy
  and then invoices them for duty and the transfer fee. DoT's *Buy a vehicle*
  page puts it plainly: "When you buy a licensed vehicle from a car dealer, it
  is the dealer's responsibility to complete the steps to transfer the vehicle
  licence into your name." The 14-day deadline is the **private-sale MR9**
  obligation (and the Road Traffic (Vehicles) Act 2012 s10(2) notice duty behind
  it, which our MR9B lodgement discharges on the buyer's behalf). It does not
  apply to a dealer sale.
- It collects driver's licence number, date of birth and residential address.
  The form states it is classed **OFFICIAL SENSITIVE** once completed — more
  weight behind not letting this data sit in an inbox.

For **new** bikes there is no transfer — the licence is issued for the first
time, which is VL12 / VL-E79 territory rather than MR9B. And on Dealer Online
neither form is produced: DOS issues the registration directly. MR9B is
therefore not on the critical path for the online model at all.

**Identity: DoT's standard is a record match, not a face match.** VL186 states
what proof of identity a vehicle licence application needs:

> You will need to prove your identity by providing one of the following:
> • A Western Australian driver's licence number; **or**
> • Primary (eg. Passport, Birth Certificate) and secondary documents.

For anyone who already holds a WA licence, the number *is* the proof — checked
against DoT's own records. That is why Dealer Online can transact without anyone
sighting a document, and it is the root of the impersonation gap. See
`../online_licensing.md` for what that means for us and what closes it.

**On the 15% figure:** older copies of these regulations show clause 8.2 as
**15%**. That was correct until two amendments changed it:

- **1 January 2021** — the pre-estimated damages cap was cut from 15% to **5%**
  of the total purchase price.
- **7 June 2024** — the genuine-estimate requirement was added as a new clause
  8.3, renumbering the deposit-application clause to 8.4 (industry bulletin 20).

The consolidation stored here is the current one and already reflects both.
Verified verbatim in this file:

> 8.2 If this Contract is validly terminated by the Dealer, the Dealer may seek
> an amount up to, but not exceeding, **5%** of the Total Purchase Price of the
> Vehicle as pre‑estimated damages.
> 8.3 Any amount sought by the Dealer under Clause 8.2 must be a **genuine
> estimate of the Dealer's loss** arising from the Purchaser's breach…

Clauses 1.3 (offer withdrawable, lapses next business day), 3.1 (balance payable
on delivery) and 4.2 (three months for a new vehicle) are unchanged from the
older consolidation. Consumer Protection's guidance gives licensing costs,
accessories fitted, and demonstrable administration costs as examples of genuine
loss.

**Is anything newer?** Checked 21 Aug 2026: no. The legislation.wa.gov.au
currency table lists 03-j0-00 (7 Jun 2024) as the only consolidation, with
currency end "Current", and Consumer Protection's vehicle sales contracts page
was last updated 21 Nov 2024 with no notice of later change. A reform pipeline
does exist — the combined review of the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act 1973 and Motor
Vehicle Repairers Act 2003 is complete and amendments to the Acts are to be
drafted for Parliament, with further regulation amendments to follow — but
nothing is enacted. Re-check the currency table annually; the version suffix in
the filenames above makes a change obvious.
