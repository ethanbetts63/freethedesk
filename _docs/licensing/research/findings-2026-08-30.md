# Research findings — 30 Aug 2026

Answers to open questions 2, 6, 7, 8 and 14. Sourced from the MV Dealers (Sales)
Regulations 1974 consolidation in `../wa_dealer_forms/`, Consumer Protection WA,
Transport WA and Stripe docs. Not legal advice.

## 1. Two hard deadlines nobody has accounted for

**Schedule 5 cl 1.3 — the offer lapses.** The purchaser's signature is an
*offer*, not a contract. Verbatim:

> The offer of the Purchaser may be withdrawn by the Purchaser any time before
> it is accepted by the Dealer. It will automatically lapse at the close of
> business on the next normal business day for motor vehicle dealers.

A contract exists only when the dealer signs **and** gives notice of acceptance
(cl 1.2). So a customer who signs on Friday night and a dealer who countersigns
on Tuesday have **no contract** — the offer died Monday at close of business.

This is the single most important product finding. An async remote flow makes
this failure mode routine, where a counter sale makes it nearly impossible. The
system must escalate the dealer hard inside that window, record the notice of
acceptance as its own event, and show the customer a live offer status. Nothing
in `contract_special_conditions.md` mentions it.

**MR9B — 7 days from sale**, blue copy to DVS, $100 infringement for missing it.
A second clock, on the dealer, on the used path only.

## 2. The used path is simpler than assumed (Q6)

MR9B is a *notification of change of ownership* lodged within 7 days **of sale**.
DoT does not require proof of payment in full, and nothing requires the transfer
to precede delivery — DTMI invoices the purchaser for duty and transfer fees
afterwards.

**So SC5 has no basis on the used path.** Its justification — DoT will only
accept proof of ownership showing paid in full, and the vehicle must be licensed
before delivery — is a *first licensing* problem. A used bike is already
licensed. Prescribed cl 3.1 (balance on delivery) works unmodified, which also
sidesteps open question 1 in `contract_special_conditions.md` entirely on this
path.

Schedule 5 already distinguishes new from used on its own:

- **cl 4.3** — used vehicle with no delivery date stated: **1 month** (new is 3
  months under cl 4.2).
- **cl 3.2 and 3.3** — manufacturer price adjustment and the 5% cap apply "In
  the case of a new Vehicle" only. Used prices are fixed. The "price hold"
  concern in the draft is a new-stock issue only.

Confirms SC3 and SC4 are new-stock clauses. They should be conditional on stock
type, not present in every contract.

**But there is a new problem.** MR9B must be signed by **both** dealer and
purchaser, and it is a two-part carbon form — blue seller's copy, red
purchaser's copy. A carbon form cannot be produced digitally. So on the used
path, either DVS accepts a printed substitute bearing an electronic signature
(open question 4, unresolved), or the dealer prints and handles it manually, or
the dealer must be on Dealer Online. **The used path may not be fully automatable
for dealers without DOS.** Worth confirming with DVS in the same call as Q4.

## 3. There is no cooling-off period in WA (Q8)

Confirmed with Consumer Protection WA: no cooling-off applies to vehicle sales.
Once the dealer accepts, the purchaser is bound. Their only exits are cl 7.1
(dealer breach), the statutory warranty, and the ACL.

This makes the sight-unseen used sale materially riskier than assumed. A buyer
who has never seen the bike is committed the moment the dealer countersigns,
with no statutory escape. A stronger special condition is the right instinct,
but note what it can and cannot do: an ACL guarantee cannot be excluded, and a
condition that tries to shut down complaints about condition will not survive.
The workable shape is a **positive** one — a dated photographic condition report
and declared defects schedule incorporated into the contract, plus a
contractual right to reject on delivery for undisclosed material defects. That
gives the buyer a real remedy and gives the dealer a documented record of what
was represented. Both sides are better off than with a disclaimer.

Cancellation damages are capped at **5% of the total purchase price** (cl 8.2)
and must be a genuine estimate (cl 8.3).

## 4. The statutory warranty rule (Q7)

Established, deterministic, and automatable. **Regulation 7** sets the duty:

> (1) **Before** a dealer sells a vehicle to which the obligations imposed by
> section 34 will apply, the dealer must ensure that the purchaser is given an
> information statement … in the form of Form 5A [with] (ii) if the vehicle is a
> motor cycle — the illustration … entitled "The Used Bike Warranty".
>
> (2) **Before** a dealer sells a vehicle to which the obligations imposed by
> section 34 will not apply, the dealer must ensure that the purchaser is given
> an information statement in the form of Form 6.

Every used sale gets one or the other, and **before the sale** — so the warranty
statement must be shown before the customer signs, not bundled into the pack
afterwards. That is a sequencing constraint on the flow.

The test (confirmed against both the regs and the current Consumer Protection
guidance — all three conditions must hold):

| | Motorcycle | Car |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash price incl. GST | ≥ $3,500 | ≥ $4,000 |
| Age | ≤ 8 years | ≤ 12 years |
| Odometer | ≤ 80,000 km | ≤ 180,000 km |

Motorcycles are a single tier: if all three hold, **3 months or 5,000 km**,
whichever comes first. Otherwise Form 6. Cars tier — ≤10 yrs and ≤150,000 km
gives 3 months / 5,000 km; over that but within the outer limits gives 1 month /
1,500 km.

Related: **reg 5** requires the Form 4 notice to state "whether the vehicle is
one to which the obligations imposed by section 34 will apply", plus VIN, engine
number, licence expiry, make/model, repairable write-off status and the dealer's
stock number. **Reg 8** requires copies of the notice after sale to carry the
date of sale and the odometer reading at the time of sale. **Reg 6** — Form 5 is
only needed where defects are being excluded. **Reg 14** — $2,000 penalty for
contravening any of it.

**Version warning.** All prescribed forms were reissued **July–August 2025**;
the regs consolidation we hold is 7 Jun 2024. The thresholds are unchanged, but
the product needs a form-version check as a standing task, not a one-off.

## 5. Stripe Identity can show the dealer the licence image (Q14)

Yes, and in the right shape. A VerificationReport carries reference IDs for the
collected images, retrievable through the FileUpload API. Stripe's own
recommendation:

> do not download the file contents on your server — send the FileLink URL to
> the client to display the image.

So the dealer views the licence in their portal, the image is served from
Stripe, and freethedesk never stores it. That satisfies the dealer's need to see
it, satisfies SC10's consent, and keeps the retention liability off our
infrastructure. Requires **Files Write** permission alongside the Identity read
permissions.

## 6. The editable-clauses mitigation has a sharp edge (Q2)

Letting dealers add and edit their own special conditions is the right call —
the dealer chooses, so we are supplying a tool rather than advising. But note
what WA joined on **1 July 2022**: the Legal Profession Uniform Law. Section 10
prohibits an unqualified entity from engaging in legal practice — 250 penalty
units or 2 years — and adds a consequence that matters more to a subscription
business than the fine:

> an entity is not entitled to recover any amount, and must repay any amount
> received, in respect of anything the entity did in contravention

Repaying subscription revenue is an existential outcome, not a cost of doing
business.

**But the restriction is narrow.** Writing contract terms — including our own
default special conditions — is unrestricted. So is distributing them as a
template, and so is filling a prescribed form with a dealer's data. Applying a
bright-line statutory test with no discretion (the warranty threshold engine) is
automation, not practice, the same way payroll software applying award rates is
not practice. The line is **advising a specific dealer which clause suits their
circumstances**.

What makes freethedesk worth designing carefully rather than worrying about: we
vary a *prescribed* form, which is narrower ground than generic templates — SC5
tries to displace prescribed cl 3.1, and whether it can is still open. That is
worth a lawyer's eyes regardless of the practice question.

Settled model in `../open-questions.md` Q2: one reviewed default set, per-clause
approval at onboarding, dealers free to remove or add with the deviation
recorded, no review of their edits, no clause library with guidance.
